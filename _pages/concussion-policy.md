# **<u>DCGFFL Concussion Protocol</u>**

&nbsp;

### **Observed by Captains or Game Officials**

This may include any noticeable changes in a player's demeanor or responsiveness, indicating a potential injury.

* **Dazed or Stunned**\- The athlete may appear disoriented, as if in a daze, and may not respond appropriately to questions or commands.
* **Confused About Assignments or Plays**\- The individual struggles to recall their role during the game, often appearing puzzled about which position to take or what play is being executed.
* **Forgets Instructions-** The player may have difficulty remembering recent directions given by coaches or teammates, suggesting impaired memory function.
* **Clumsy Movements or Poor Balance-** The athlete might display uncoordinated movements, stumbling or having trouble maintaining their balance, which can be a clear indicator of a concussion.
* **Changes in Mood, Behavior, or Personality-** Sudden emotional shifts, increased irritability, or unusual behaviors can signal that something is wrong, as concussions can affect mental state.

### **Symptoms of Concussion Reported by Players**

* **Headache or Pressure in the Head-** Players often describe a persistent headache or a sensation of pressure that feels different from a regular headache.
* **Nausea or Vomiting-** Some may experience feelings of nausea, which can sometimes lead to vomiting, indicating significant distress.
* **Balance Problems or Dizziness-** Individuals might struggle to maintain their balance or feel dizzy, making it difficult for them to walk or stand straight.
* **Sensitivity to Light or Noise-** A common symptom is an increased sensitivity to bright lights or loud sounds, which can cause discomfort or exacerbate feelings of unease.
* **Feeling “Not Right” or “Down-** Many players report an overall sense of feeling unwell, emotionally or physically, describing a sensation of being “off” or experiencing a low mood.
* ### **If there is a suspicion of a concussion in an athlete**

1. **Immediate Removal-** The athlete must be taken out of the game or practice right away to prevent further risk of injury.
2. **Professional Evaluation-** It is crucial that the athlete undergoes a thorough evaluation by a qualified medical professional who is experienced in assessing concussions.
3. **Notification of Emergency Contact-** Ensure that the athlete’s emergency contact is promptly informed about the potential concussion so they can stay informed and involved in the recovery process.
4. **Rest and Recovery-** The athlete should not participate in any further play on the day of the injury and must remain sidelined until he or she receives medical clearance from a qualified professional, ensuring their well-being and safety.
